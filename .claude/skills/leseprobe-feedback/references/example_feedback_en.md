# Phrasing bank and example (English theses)

## Phrasing bank

**Overall impression**
- The sample covers … and gives a solid / convincing / still developing overall impression.
- A particular strength is …
- The text shows that you have engaged closely with …

**Research question**
- The research question is clearly stated and consistently pursued in Section ….
- In Section … the link to the research question gets lost; this part remains largely descriptive.
- The sample does not state an explicit research question. Formulate it in the introduction so that every chapter can be aligned with it.
- For each section, check what it contributes to answering the research question.

**Line of argument**
- The line of argument is easy to follow; the sections build on each other logically.
- A transition between … and … is missing, so the argument jumps.
- Short interim conclusions at the end of subsections would pull the argument together.
- The claim "…" is central to your argument but is not supported by a source.
- The term … is used before it is defined / is used inconsistently.

**Sources**
- The literature base is current and extensive.
- The argument relies mainly on textbooks and websites; add relevant peer-reviewed journal articles.
- Key literature from the last five years is largely missing.
- Studies are listed one after another rather than compared critically (especially at Master level).

**Citation**
- Citation follows APA 7 consistently.
- Page numbers are missing for some direct quotations.
- Several claims (e.g. on p. …) are not referenced; this also needs to be avoided for plagiarism reasons.
- Key works are cited only secondarily ("as cited in"); consult the originals.

**Grammar and style**
- The text reads fluently and is largely error-free.
- Use either British or American spelling consistently.
- Avoid contractions (e.g. "don't", "wasn't") and colloquial phrasing such as "…".
- Some sentences follow German word order or are very long; split or restructure them.

**Next steps**
- As next steps I recommend: first …, second …, third ….

## Example (Master thesis, literature review, approx. 240 words)

The sample covers the literature review of your Master thesis on the effect of sustainability claims on brand trust. The chapter is well structured, and the definitions of greenwashing and brand trust in Section 2.1 are precise and well referenced.

However, the link to your research question remains implicit. Section 2.2 on the history of corporate social responsibility is largely descriptive and contributes little to answering it; shorten it and focus on the constructs you will measure later. Between Sections 2.3 and 2.4 a transition is missing, so the argument jumps from consumer scepticism to message framing. A short interim conclusion at the end of the chapter would show which hypotheses follow from the literature.

The literature base is current and largely consists of peer-reviewed journal articles, which is appropriate for a Master thesis. The studies, however, are mostly summarised one after another; compare their findings and discuss contradictions. Some direct quotations lack page numbers, and the market figure on p. 14 is not referenced.

The text reads fluently overall. Please use either British or American spelling consistently, avoid contractions such as "don't", and check subject–verb agreement in longer sentences. Several sentences follow German word order and would read more naturally if restructured.

As next steps I recommend: first, make the contribution of each section to the research question explicit; second, discuss the studies critically rather than summarising them; third, add the missing page numbers and references.
